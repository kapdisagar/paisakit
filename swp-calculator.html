How SWP is calculated

Every month, your balance first grows by the monthly return, then the withdrawal is deducted:

B(n) = P × (1 + r)n − W × [((1 + r)n − 1) / r]

where P is the corpus, W the monthly withdrawal and r the monthly rate (annual rate ÷ 12). The calculator simulates this month by month.

Worked example: ₹25,00,000 corpus, ₹15,000/month withdrawal at 8% p.a. — first-month interest is ₹16,667, more than the withdrawal, so the balance grows: roughly ₹25.2 lakh after 1 year and ₹28.1 lakh after 10 years. But withdraw ₹20,000/month instead and the corpus runs out in about 22.5 years (270 months).

FAQ

SWP kya hai — FD se better hai monthly income ke liye?

SWP means withdrawing a fixed amount from a mutual fund every month. Long-term returns are usually higher than FD, and tax can be lower too (only the gains part of each withdrawal is taxed at capital-gains rates, not the full interest at slab rate). The trade-off: returns are not guaranteed — markets can dip, especially early on.

Kitna withdrawal safe maana jaata hai?

A common rule of thumb: keep annual withdrawals around 4–6% of the corpus. If your monthly withdrawal is less than the monthly interest earned (like ₹15,000 vs ₹16,667 interest on ₹25 lakh at 8%), the corpus never depletes. Withdraw more than that and it starts shrinking.

Expected return kitna assume karun?

Be conservative. Hybrid/balanced funds: 7–9%. Equity funds: 10–12% long-term average, but the early years can be volatile — if markets fall right after you start, a heavy withdrawal drains the corpus faster (sequence-of-returns risk). Many people withdraw less in the first few years.

SWP par tax kaise lagta hai?

Each withdrawal has two parts: your own capital (no tax) and gains (taxable). For equity funds: 12.5% LTCG on gains above ₹1.25 lakh/year if held over 12 months, 20% STCG otherwise. Debt funds are taxed at your slab rate. This calculator shows pre-tax numbers — actual in-hand amount will differ.
